## Prologue - Valleys

The short time I have spent trail running has been intersected by a bordering obsession for adventure in the Tararua. I knew I had to unlock more of these hills by covering the greater distance running, it seemed there was already a community that lives this reason. So a big shoutout to BSR and everyone who planted the seed to do this run.

History says sometime in 1878, while out prospecting for gold my Great-Great-Great Grandfather Richard “Dicky” Sayer, accidentally set fire to the bush creating some of the most runnable sections of the SK Valleys- the river flat alongside the Waiohine that we know today as Tōtara Flats.

Reflecting on his toils whilst tracing his trials along the Waiohine. I spared a thought for his determination as I made strides to conquer the Valleys route.

I hope this “blow-by-blow” account could one day be treated as a prologue for future reports pending any SK Main Range or Tarn (…or Carkeek) I should attempt. I’ll try avoid any sensationalist words and keep it brief knowing there are bigger things to achieve in the mountains. Only adding to the great SK story, I’ve most appreciated reading these write-ups and hope I may glean a little more wisdom for the 92nd SKV member.

## Ruamāhanga

The thought of navigating the river section filled me with anticipation and anxiety, I’d heard conflicting stories of both great success and swims. This was unfamiliar territory, and no statistic or data could describe the experience. 

To my surprise, I encountered three fellow SK Valley trampers deliberating a river crossing of the Ruamāhanga. It made me wonder why this route has never made the cult “Tararua Classic” tramp status, they were out for an epic few days in some beautifully diverse valleys.

Together, we worked out a river crossing and a path forward; was a river sidle to be? I set off (giving them the only dive signal I know- ‘Okay’) and after a few unnecessary crossings, concluded that sticking to the true right was the wisest choice for most of the journey, the waters warmed, and in the end I was a bit saddened this valley concluded just as I reached Cleft Creek…my croissants for lunch a lil’ soggy from some swims.

## Waiāwangawanga

Cow Saddle Cow Sign and Slippery Plank, two infamous Valley features to look forward too! I was lil’ disappointed in the amount of time I’d spent on the Ruamāhanga, and when reaching Cow Creek I was too disappointed to find I hadn't packed any electrolyte. Undeterred, I buckled in for this next section, I had plenty of food, and found the remaining dregs of my pack- some “expired” salt pills and weird fizzy electrolyte tabs (don’t recommend).

Dancing along the undulations of the Waingawa my mind was wandering. I was working with very little sleep here. This was not to be the light post New Years antics recovery jog! A sea shanty (only it didn’t really rhyme) played persistently in the fatigued mind. I got to Mitre Flats Hut cluttered with belongings, however all the people were out for their own adventures in pursuit of a nearby 1500. 

## Te Whakatūrākau (Atiwhakatū)

The ultimate test of the legs - the run along the Atiwhakatū super highway! Arriving at the hut, I noted my friend Cain (and ride home) on a Holdsworth-Jumbo recce logged in just 30mins prior. The hut tank was empty; I popped on some dry socks, and as soon as I enjoyed the comforts of good foot care, I dunked them in the river fetching water - Good one Callum! I was in my happy place, and despite the relentless sidling so far, it felt great to reach this point. Arguably the route gets a lot easier, a mantra that I’d doggedly decided to keep close for when the suffer hit.


## Wai-o-Hine Wairaka

A quick message dispatched at Pig Flat, my ETA to the Carpark of Glory trailing behind about an hour. These splits were a pretty inexact science and I should apologise to the countless day walkers seeking information- “Which way to X?” or  “Oh, how far to blah-blah?”, only giving them some pretty rubbish times, and in growing bitterness, head was down, I chose to simply ignore these signs of civilisation going forward.

Tōtara Flats seemed a heaving metropolis with endless accommodation options along the stunning Waiohine. There would be no stopping for a micro-nap though! At the hut, I called out “nice shirt” spotting someone in an elusive SK tee, the very jersey I was out to earn, only it seems this garment could be purchased off the shelf, and I could just call it a day and join the fashionable!

I was ready to put in the hard mahi; now well and truely within the pain-cave. A last minute addition to my gear arsenal, a tube of Gurney Goo. An awkward moment was had raising a few eye brows as I stood on the side of the track “tending to the chafe”.

## Tauwharenīkau

With earphones on, I had the motivational running playlist set to soundtrack this next leg, and the longest valley to come. Cone Saddle felt less formidable than I anticipated, my legs had been longing for this slow-burn climb; it was almost pleasant! At least compared to the Barton Track earlier, I felt it was. Trying with some desperation to stick to the mockery “20mInS tO CoNE HuT” DOC sign (~23mins) I dug deep relieved to arrive at the idyllic setting, occupants having dinner al fresco.

The familiar home stretch unfolded and I was not expecting any Strava segment laurels at this point. Running down the catchment became an emotional blur as I passed endless people enjoying the serene evening in the Tararua. Cone Hut, Tutuwai picnic table, cricket wicket, and Smith Creek- I reminisced on the day that had just gone, connected with my “why?” tapping the sign at 17hrs 30mins.
