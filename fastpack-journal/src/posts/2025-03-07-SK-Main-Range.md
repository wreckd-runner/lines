It was sometime between the 26th of March and the 27th of May, 2020, that I discovered my
affinity for trail running. I had already been spending a significant amount of time in
my happy place, the Tararua, tramping about the lower reaches. I took light of an
entry in the Field Hut log book. Along the
lines of “Right for shopping - aborted
SK”. Cryptic! But soon I discovered Big
Sunday Runs (BSR), and despite having no
significant wilderness experience and
feeling somewhat apprehensive, I joined a
Tuesday night SX run. Over a beer at the
pub, I first flirted with the idea of an
SK Traverse.

An SK quickly became a life goal.
Adventures in the hills, such as the Three
Kings Loop or SMR, were really just recce
runs—sections of the Tarn and Main Range
routes. After completing the Valleys—a prelude with a personal connection to the Tararua—
I realised that any travel along the tops would be a very different beast. It wasn’t
going to be easy. It was going to be a test in self-preservation, long, and ultimately
change my perspective on what is hard. I made the decision to get it done. When the time
came, I’d know when I was ready. Still, I was largely indecisive about which flavour of
SK to tackle next.

Finding the right window for the SK was frustrating. It became my greatest competing
priority, and I was constantly blocking dates in the calendar for the attempt. After a
pretty epic summer of running and biking missions, I still felt undertrained. But no
matter what I was doing, all I could think about was finally ticking the SK off my
adventure matrix. Even after crossing the finish line at the Old Ghost Ultra a fortnight
prior—an epic race in its own right—I was still left longing for something more, perhaps
a bit less runnable, maybe a little more hardcore! Martini—who needs no introduction to
these trip reports—was always keeping a close eye on the weather (the first tab of
windy.com... only the first tab). I Noted his despondency to the possibility of a season
void of any Main Range certifications, and when he found an open slot that was just too
irresistible to pass up, the appointment was booked!

"The Tararuas don’t wait for weekends." The trails
 were in perfect condition, and whatever excuses were
 available if I dropped out, the forecast wasn’t one
 of them, nor were the dry conditions underfoot. It
 was pretty warm, but given the alternative Tararua
 weather, it made for a special day.
 “The season is never over.” I had to get the SK done
 before the end of the financial year. It couldn’t be
 accrued, and it would be the icing on my Q4
 Adventure Report. Besides, the closer it got to the
 end of the month and the auditors showing up at
 work, the more the window for a rudely spontaneous
 day off was closing.

 The day before the mission, I felt like a kid on Christmas Eve—like the excitement of
heading off on holiday. I prepped all my nutrition and packed my bag days in advance, and
I was grateful for that.
The night before, though, I had a meeting booked that I should’ve postponed. But all that
mattered was the SK- I could think rationally later. I was very short on sleep, and I
felt I wouldn’t even make it through a parkrun in such a deficit without the help of some
serious caffeine and, of course, the other motivating factor: the curated playlist! I
packed my earphones, but when I arrived at Putara, I realised they weren’t in their case.
Considering all the things I could’ve left behind (hopefully nothing else forgotten) I
was unfazed.

SK logistics can be a challenge, but that’s part of the fun.
Disappointment can arise when a commitment is made more than a
couple of days out. Getting to Putara Road for a 4 a.m. start and
  being picked up at Kaitoke at a similar hour wouldn’t happen
                        without a close-knit community of like-minded runners who want to
                        see each other succeed—and, of course, it all comes with good
                        karma for their own adventures, and the juicy intel on whom might
                        be out there next! Huge thanks to Brendan Varty for the
                        SK AirBnB services and to Sean Martin for the SK Uber services.
                        I’d originally planned to keep the mission anonymous, but I
                        figured the logistics would be easier if I went public—and, let’s
                        be honest, I’d be held to account! Plus, people love watching the
                        dot, especially on a Friday from their desks.
                        (Don’t watch the dot, be the dot, though!)

4 a.m. arrived, and I didn’t have a specific goal in mind beyond
                        breaking 24 hours (and claiming the first certificate of the
                        season). I reached Herepai Hut in 1:03 and was wired, then made
                        my way to East Peak. I stopped longer than necessary to take in
                        the most impressive sunrise—something I’d been looking forward to
                        the most, and the main reason for the 4 a.m. start instead of an
                        evening one. I shared a few photos with the poor souls working,
                        who probably didn’t appreciate it as much as I did.
I reached Arête in 6 hours, feeling good about the progress so far. The section between
Pukematawai and Junction Knob was my greatest unknown. It was here that I made a rookie
mistake by navigating to the Kelleher highpoint—a cliché error that's been etched into
the Strava heatmap. Without any specific split expectations, I consulted Mission HQ for
intel. They gave good motivation from Dracophyllum—at least until I grew disinterested in
the splits and memes.
I stopped at Anderson’s to replenish and spent what felt like an eternity forcing down an
entire OSM bar. By this point, fatigue was setting in, but I was on familiar trail. I
knew once I reached Aokaparangi, things would get easier (spoiler: they didn’t). I was
already beyond tired, so I stopped just before Maungahuka to take one of the two NoDoze
tablets I’d packed, hoping the boost would kick in by the ladder—this being the last
place I wanted to make slumber.
I kept moving, operating on the assumption that this was a sub-24-hour mission or
nothing. I figured if I was pushing past 17.5 hours by the time I reached Kime, it was
game over, and it’d be “right for shopping”. At this point, I was making up arbitrary
times on the fly to suit my own narrative. I scraped by with 20 minutes to spare, and the
bonus PR split climbing up to Hector kept me motivated. After that, it was all downhill,
and I reached Alpha Hut with a couple of extra minutes. I used them to swap the head
torch battery and take care of myself with the final NoDoze to see me through the
Marchant- Boo!

I was a bit disappointed with my time to Block XVI; I was feeling a little worn. Still,
somehow, the fun factor increased along the Marchant Ridge—at least until the final
descent, when I had an epic fall on a gnarly tree root. It threw me two meters down the
track, and for a moment, I thought it was game over. But there was no time to check for
blood, missing appendages or anything that might’ve fallen from my pack. So onwards!
It was only at this point, I knew I’d make it safely inside 24 hours. I was considering
the matter of how much time I’d have to spare, and whether I cared enough to push
further. When the track from the Dobson turnoff was so unusually dry, I decided to run a
bit and make the most of it. I reached the Carpark of Glory in 23 hours and 48
minutes.
